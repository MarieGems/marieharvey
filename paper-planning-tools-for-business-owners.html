Paper Planning Tools for a Scatterbrained Business Owner

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · October 6, 2026

I get scatterbrained a lot, and the four paper tools I would point to on my Amazon lists are aimed at exactly that: a floral spiral journal, a purple A5 leather journal, a seven pack of gel pens, and a magnetic acrylic fridge calendar. Paper will not run your business for you, but it gives your head somewhere to put things down.

Is paper planning the same as desk organization?

Not quite, and I want to be clear about it. This post is about organizing your week and your thoughts, not about sorting the clutter on a desk. My storefront does not have a list of desk organizers I can honestly point to, so I am sticking to the paper tools that are on my lists: two from Overwhelmed Mom Brain Essentials and two from my Lavender Gift Set.

Why does paper still make sense for planning?

Paper is the one planning tool that never sends a notification. When a lot is open at once, writing something down and closing the page can be easier than managing another app. It also has history in my work: Marie Gems started with stickers and bullet journals before it became a DTF transfer shop, and I wrote about that shift in how Marie Gems actually changed. My Pinterest Marketing Tools list on Benable has an undated daily content planner notebook, and my note on it says digital calendars are great until your app crashes mid-brainstorm, so I still map out pin themes and client content on paper first.

Which journal should you choose?

Decide whether you want a working journal or a nice one. The Tizzer floral brain spiral journal is a spiral-bound journal with a floral cover, the kind of notebook you can fill up and not feel precious about. The CAGIE purple A5 leather journal is a purple leather journal in A5, which is roughly 5.8 by 8.3 inches, a size that fits in a bag. Check each listing for the material, paper, page count, and whether it lays flat, because those details decide how the journal feels to write in.

Do gel pens make a difference for planning?

A pen you like makes you more likely to pick up the journal at all. The thinkor black gel pens come as a seven pack, which is enough to leave one at the desk, one in your bag, and one wherever you end up making lists. Gel ink moves smoothly across the page, and plain black keeps a planner readable at a glance. If you tend to lose pens, a multipack does more for you than a single nice pen.

Why put a calendar on the fridge?

A fridge is where everyone in the house already looks. The DIYMAG magnetic acrylic fridge calendar is sold as a four pack, and being magnetic means it sticks to the fridge or any steel surface without a hole in the wall. A household with seven kids has a lot of schedules, and they are easier to follow when they live in one visible place instead of in one person's head. A shared calendar also means the question of who needs to be where can be answered by looking up.

What should you write in a planning journal first?

Start with a weekly reset instead of a perfect system. Write the three things that must happen this week, then the appointments that are already fixed, then everything that is floating around in your head. That is enough to make the journal useful on day one, and you can add structure once you know what you actually use. A journal that asks for too much on the first page tends to get abandoned by the third.

How do these four work together?

Use each for a different job so they do not compete. The spiral journal is for daily notes and to-do lists, the leather journal is for bigger planning like goals and ideas, the pens are for both, and the fridge calendar is for what the whole household needs to know. If you keep the jobs separate, you always know where a thing belongs. The bottom line on paper planning tools

Pick the one that matches the gap you feel most: a place to dump thoughts, a place to plan, or a place everyone can see the week. If you want the longer story of how a scattered brain, two businesses, and seven kids share one day, how the days actually work is the honest version.
