Why My Pinterest List Pin Showed the Same Number Twice

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · November 13, 2026

My Pinterest list pin showed the same number twice, and once I actually looked at it, the mistake was obvious: a pin titled "2 Search Intents Your Small Business SEO Must Target" had a big standalone "2" as its design element, and the headline also opened with the word "2." On October 23, 2026, I caught it on a live Marie Glow Studio pin and asked for two changes. Here is what I saw, why it happened, and the second change that came out of looking at the same pin.

What was actually wrong with the pin?

The same digit appeared twice in two different places doing the same job. A list-style pin design already carries a big numeral to show how many items the list has. This pin's headline also opened with that same number written out, so a reader saw "2" as a large design element and then read "2 Search Intents" right next to it. It read as a mistake, not a style choice.

Why did the headline repeat the number the design already showed?

Because the rule for writing a list pin's headline had never said not to. The big numeral on a list-archetype pin is meant to carry the count on its own, so the headline is supposed to lead with the noun instead, something like "Search Intents Your Small Business SEO Must Target." Nothing in the pin-writing instructions said that outright, so the habit of starting a headline with its own number had never been caught.

What exactly did I ask for?

I said, in close to these words, for future pins remove the second number, and make sure every pin has a photo and no plain cards. The first part fixed the specific pin in front of me. The second part was a bigger standing change I decided on while looking at the same pin.

Why did looking at one pin lead to a second, bigger change?

Because the flat, photo-less look on that pin was not actually necessary anymore. Marie Glow Studio's Pinterest pipeline had a policy that most pins could stay flat, treated as normal, going back to when the pin designs first launched in late September 2026. By October 23, the confirmed photo library behind that pipeline had grown to about 110 entries, far more than existed when the flat-by-default policy was written. The policy was built for a photo shortage that no longer existed.

What is the rule now for whether a pin gets a photo?

Every future pin gets one. Flat is a fallback only, for the rare case where the confirmed photo library genuinely has nothing that fits a specific pin, and even then it should be flagged as a gap in the photo library rather than treated as a normal outcome.

Did this fix touch pins that had already gone out?

No. Both changes apply to every batch going forward, starting the day they were made. Pins already scheduled or published before October 23 were left as they were.

Could this same mistake happen on a different brand's pins?

Yes, since any list-style pin on any brand carries the same big numeral and the same temptation to repeat it in the headline. The headline rule was written into the shared instructions that every brand's list pins draw from, not only Marie Glow Studio's, so the same mistake should not show up again on a different brand's list pin either.

The bottom line on a Pinterest list pin number

A design mistake caught on one pin is worth asking a second question about: is the policy behind it still the right one, or was it built for a situation that no longer applies? The headline fix solved what I actually saw. The photo policy change came from asking that second question about the same pin.
