The Web Design Tools and Business Planners I Actually Use

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · November 2, 2026

The web design tools I actually use to run Marie Glow Studio and the rest of my work live on one Benable list: software for building a site, design tools, client planners, and the paper planners that back all of it up when an app crashes mid brainstorm. My Web Designer's Must Haves list on Benable has 21 items, and most of them are real tools I reach for, not products with a price tag.

Disclosure: some links in this post are affiliate links, including my Benable link, and I mark it as sponsored.

What software do I actually use to build a website?

Shopify, Showit, Squarespace, Wix, and WordPress.com are all on my Web Designer's Must Haves list. I run Shopify for Marie Gems, and the rest are platforms I recommend to a client depending on what they need: Squarespace when a client wants something polished and portfolio style without heavy customization, and Wix when a client wants a beginner-friendly, drag-and-drop option they can edit themselves later. My list has no note from me on Showit or WordPress.com beyond the name, so I am naming them as real options on the list, not ranking them against each other.

Do you use the same platform for Marie Gems as for a client site?

No. Shopify runs Marie Gems itself, while a client's platform depends on what they need, not on what I personally prefer. That is the whole reason five different platforms sit on the same list instead of one.

Which design tools do I reach for before I touch code?

Coolors and Canva, both for the same reason: settling on a look before the build starts. Coolors is the color palette generator on my list, and Canva covers the rest of the visual decisions. Adobe is on the same list. None of the three carries a note from me beyond the name, so this is the honest shortlist, not a ranked review.

How do I keep a bigger client project from running away from me?

With a paper planner made for that exact job. My note on the Project Planner Notebook says it is good for breaking a bigger client project into steps and checking things off as you go. My note on the Desk Pad Calendar says a big-picture view of the month is nice to have next to the monitor for spotting deadlines at a glance, and the Weekly Planner is a simple undated weekly layout that my note says is for tracking client deadlines and to-dos without being tied to a specific start date.

What keeps client work from falling through the cracks day to day?

HoneyBook for the official record of a project, Notion for notes and project pages, and sticky notes for the moment-to-moment reminders. HoneyBook is on my list for managing client flow: proposals, contracts, and the paper trail of a project. My note on the Desk Sticky Notes says they are handy for jotting quick reminders or to-dos next to the monitor without opening another app. Linda Tong Planners are also on this list, and paper planning tools for a scatterbrained business owner covers a different set of planner picks from a different list, if paper planning is what you are after.

What does my actual desk setup look like?

A laptop, a printer, and a chair rated for a full workday. My list links to HP's laptop and printer pages, to Amazon's Home Office Desk Chairs category, and to a Marble Desk. None of those four carries a note from me, so I am naming them as real items on my list rather than endorsing one model over another. My home office must-haves covers the standing desk and the rest of my actual office setup from a different list, if you want more than these four.

Is Flodesk part of this toolkit too?

Yes. Flodesk is on the Web Designer's Must Haves list, and I already wrote the fuller version of how I use it for email in Flodesk for a small shop, so I am not repeating that here.

The bottom line on web design tools I actually use

Start with the platform that matches the client, decide the look with Coolors and Canva before the build starts, and keep a paper planner for the plan an app cannot hold onto when it crashes mid brainstorm.
