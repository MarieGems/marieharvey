Tumbler Press for Sublimation: Matching 20, 30, 40 oz

← Back to Random Thoughts

A tumbler press for sublimation has to match the tumbler's size, so choose the blanks and the press together. On my Sublimation Starter Kit list, the HTVRONT A200 press is listed for 11 to 30 oz tumblers, a separate mug press is for cups 2.9 to 3.2 inches across, and a 40 oz tumbler with a handle is covered by a separate attachment. Tumblers were part of Marie Gems before DTF became its focus, and the description I wrote for that list says it holds my favorite sublimation equipment, heat presses, and essential supplies for beginners and growing small businesses.

Which tumbler press fits a 20 or 30 oz tumbler?

The HTVRONT A200 tumbler press is the one on my list built for that range. Its listing says 11 to 30 oz, with adjustable pressure and a switchable heat plate attachment, and it names 15, 16, 20, and 30 oz tumblers and glasses. It was about $250 as I write this. Two blanks on my Amazon lists fall inside that range. The HTVRONT 20 oz sublimation tumblers come as a 20-pack with paper, straw, and box. The Muwnrol 30 oz sublimation tumblers come as a 24-pack of stainless steel, double wall vacuum cups with straws, individually boxed, at about $119, which works out to roughly $5 per tumbler. My Sublimation Must Haves list on Benable adds another 20 oz option, an 8 pack of UIRZOTN skinny sublimation tumblers, and my note on it says the 8 pack means you are not constantly reordering when you need several done at once.

Can you press a 40 oz tumbler with a handle?

You need a press that can take the handle, and on my list that is the HTVRONT tumbler press attachment for 40 oz detachable handle tumblers. Its listing says it installs and replaces in seconds and expands the machine's use range, and it was about $120 as I write this. The entry on my list does not say which press it fits, so read the compatibility details on the listing before you pair it with a machine. The 40 oz blank on my list is the AGH 40 oz white sublimation tumbler with handle, a 4-pack with a stainless steel straw and leakproof lid at about $40, or roughly $10 per tumbler.

What is the mug press for?

The HTVRONT auto tumbler and mug press is for cups with a diameter of 2.9 to 3.2 inches, and its listing names tumblers and glasses as uses. It was about $140 as I write this. It depends entirely on one measurement, so measure your blank across before you order. A cup that is a fraction of an inch outside the stated range is a cup the press was not built for.

Do you need to buy lids and straws separately?

Sometimes, because replacements are cheaper than replacing the tumbler. The XccMe replacement lids on my Business Supplies list are a 2-pack made for 30 oz straight skinny tumblers, about $7 as I write this, so confirm your blank is that shape. The ALINK reusable clear straws are a 12-pack of 10.5 inch straws for 16 to 32 oz tumblers, about $5. Check that a straw's length fits your tumbler's height, since a straw that is too short or too tall is a small problem that spoils a finished tumbler.

Why do the blanks say sublimation in the name?

Sublimation only works on polyester or poly-coated surfaces, so a tumbler has to be coated for it. That is why each blank on my list carries the word sublimation in its title, and it is also why a plain stainless tumbler will not take a sublimation design. If you are still choosing between methods, DTF vs. sublimation vs. HTV compares them, and how Marie Gems changed over three years covers where tumblers fit in my own shop's history. Follow the instructions that come with your blanks and your press for time and temperature.

How should you pick a tumbler press and blanks together?

Pick the tumbler size you expect to make most, buy blanks in that size, then buy the press whose listing names it. For 20 and 30 oz, that points to the A200 and either blank pack. For 40 oz with a handle, add the attachment and confirm it fits your press. The mug press only fits if your cup lands in its narrow diameter range. Start with the smallest blank pack, not the 24-pack, until your first few tumblers come out the way you want.

The press and the blank have to agree on size, and every listing above states its own numbers so you can check. You can open my Sublimation Starter Kit list to see all 19 items, including the presses, papers, and blanks, and my Brands page shows what I have built.
