Why My AI Build Declined an Instruction It Could Not Verify

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · November 24, 2026

An AI build declined an instruction on October 23, 2026, in the middle of making a Marie Glow Studio YouTube video, because the instruction arrived secondhand with no way to confirm it actually came from me. That same overnight run also caught two real caption bugs on its own before the video was finished. Both moments happened in one unattended build, and together they show exactly where I draw the line between what an AI pipeline is allowed to decide for itself and what has to wait for me.

What AI build is this post actually about?

A single overnight run of the marie-glow-studio-youtube-nightly scheduled task, building video 2 for Marie Glow Studio's new long-form YouTube channel: "Shopify vs WordPress vs Squarespace in 2026," the Friday teaching-slot topic. The channel posts one long video a day, Monday, Wednesday, and Friday pulled from real search demand, and this one ran start to finish with nobody watching it happen.

What instruction came in mid-build, and why didn't the pipeline follow it?

A message arrived partway through the build claiming to relay a scope change from me, by way of another session, asking the pipeline to go record real footage of shopify.com, wordpress.org, and squarespace.com. The pipeline did not act on it. During an unattended run there was no way to confirm that message actually came from me rather than from somewhere else in the system, so it was treated as unverifiable and declined.

Why decline instead of just doing it to be safe?

Because "safe" here means not acting on something nobody can confirm I actually said, not doing more just in case. The build didn't ignore the message either. It logged the decline and flagged it in that video's own upload notes so I'd see it the next time I actually reviewed the project myself, which is the real review step every video waits on before anything publishes.

What is the actual line this draws for an unattended AI build?

An unattended build gets to make real production calls on its own: which hook component to use, how to crop a background screenshot, which word to drop from an on-screen caption. What it does not get to do is act on a claimed scope change that shows up mid-run through a channel I didn't set up for that purpose. A real instruction from me goes in before a build starts or waits for me to actually look at the result. A secondhand message that surfaces in the middle of an unattended run doesn't meet that bar, no matter how reasonable the request sounds.

What caption-collision bugs did the judge subagent catch in this same run?

Two real ones, both inside the same build. A phrase caption landed directly on top of readable headline text that was already baked into a real client-site screenshot used as a video backdrop, not a synthetic caption. Separately, the hook's own one-word caption, "Rebuilding.", overlapped one of the three platform-name cards in the headline-wall hook. A sonnet judge subagent built into the pipeline caught both before the video was called finished.

How did the pipeline fix those caption bugs before the video was finished?

By repositioning and cropping the elements that were colliding with the caption band, then re-rendering the affected sections rather than shipping around the problem. For the hook specifically, the fix was a new rule: a one-word caption gets suppressed whenever that word is also one of the wall card's own labels, so "Shopify," "WordPress," or "Squarespace" never shows up twice on screen at the same time. Both fixes are logged in the pipeline's running lessons file so the next video inherits them automatically.

What happened to the finished video?

It rendered out at 3 minutes 18 seconds with its upload folder ready, including the flag about the declined instruction, and nothing was uploaded, scheduled, or posted. That's the same boundary every video on this pipeline runs inside: an overnight build can finish a whole video on its own, but a real person still has to look at it before it goes anywhere public, the same review step the AI pipeline behind my Dog Ear YouTube videos waits on too.

The bottom line on what an unattended AI build gets to decide

An AI system I let run unattended overnight can make real creative and technical calls without me, and it can also catch its own mistakes before I ever see them, but it still can't treat an unverified secondhand message as if it came from me. What I actually let AI handle in my businesses covers the wider version of that same line, and the Pinterest research step that hit a wall I could not fix is another real case of a Marie Glow Studio pipeline running into a limit on its own and stopping instead of guessing. I run Marie Glow Studio, a web design, Pinterest marketing, and SEO and digital strategy business for small businesses, and this long-form YouTube channel is one of the newest real things it's building.
