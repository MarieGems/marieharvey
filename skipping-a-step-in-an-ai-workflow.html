Skipping a Step in an AI Workflow: What It Cost Me

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · November 1, 2026

Skipping a step in an AI workflow to save time cost me a batch of flat pins on September 19, 2026. My AI assistant was building a small top-up batch of eight Pinterest pins for this site, skipped the photo-matching stage to save time and usage, and every pin came out without a photo behind it. I noticed and said so. Here is why the batch was rushed, what the missing step does, and the rule I set afterward.

Why was the eight-pin Pinterest top-up batch rushed?

Because days were short. I had pointed out that only about three pins went out on September 18, and the rule at the time was five pins a day. The cause was simple: my first hand-spaced pins had left Friday, Saturday, and Sunday at two, one, and one, and the weekly batch did not start until Monday, September 21. The fix was an eight-pin top-up that filled Saturday, September 19 and Sunday, September 20 to five pins each.

What is the photo-matching step?

It is the stage that puts a real photo behind a pin's headline. I approved that look on September 18, and it matches the pins for Marie Gems. When the stage is skipped, the pins render flat, with a headline on a plain background and no photo. That is what happened here. I asked why the pins had no photos behind them like the Marie Gems pins, since that was supposed to be set already.

Why did skipping the step seem reasonable?

Because the job was small and urgent. It was two days short and eight pins long, and a small urgent job feels like the place to trim. That is the trap. A step that is part of the normal process is part of it for a reason, and a rush is the moment you are least likely to notice what it was protecting.

What did fixing the flat pins involve?

The photo stage was run and applied, and the batch was re-rendered. The cost of skipping the step was a flat first render that had to be redone, plus me having to ask for something that was supposed to be set already. I am not claiming anything about saves or clicks. The 40 percent cap on photo pins stayed in force, so 3 of the 8 pins got photos, and one of them used a real workspace selfie in place of an illustrated Marie Glow Studio mockup, which was passed over on purpose.

What is my rule for the photo-matching step now?

The photo stage is part of every run for this site, small or urgent or neither. An individual pin may stay flat when no honest photo fits, but the stage itself is never skipped. The rule also says something about who decides: a set-up pipeline is not trimmed silently to save usage. If a step needs to go, the assistant says so and I choose.

The same day, the weekly job was changed to start with a coverage check. It counts the pins already scheduled for each day through the coming Sunday, by Central time, and tops up any day that is under five.

What should you do when an AI workflow wants to skip a step?

Make skipping a decision you make, not a default the tool makes. Ask the assistant to tell you which steps it is cutting and why before it cuts them. Then decide whether the shortcut is worth the result. I wrote about this kind of line in what I actually let AI handle in my businesses, and the pattern is the same here: the assistant does the work, and the calls about the process stay mine.

The bottom line on skipping a step in an AI workflow

The step that gets skipped is usually the one that makes the work look finished. If a job feels too small for the full process, that is the moment to keep the whole process and say so out loud.
