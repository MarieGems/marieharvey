Exactly What I Let AI Handle in My Businesses Today

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · September 19, 2026

AI drafts almost everything I publish, in words that sound like me, from a real source I can point to. It never spends my money, sends an email on its own, publishes a live storefront change, or takes a video from raw footage straight to scheduled. That's the actual line, and it's held up through months of running this across two businesses and this site.

AI drafts almost everything I publish, in my own words

Both blog pipelines, Marie Gems' Purple Print and this site's Random Thoughts, draft full posts, but neither one can publish. Pinterest pin copy across all three brands gets drafted the same way. Daily social captions for Facebook, Instagram, Threads, and Twitter get drafted five at a time. Every one of those stops at a reviewable file. None of them is allowed to hit a live button on its own unless it's already proven itself on the same routine job for weeks.

AI proposes my weekly content plan, but never schedules it

Every Friday evening, an agent drafts five to seven candidate blog and Pinterest ideas for the coming week, pulled from real sales data, a validated keyword file, and whatever's already been posted recently so nothing repeats. It never publishes or schedules a single one of them. It hands me a table, and every week it also asks me directly whether I have a real mom-life moment from that week worth folding into the plan, because that's still something only I can actually supply.

AI doesn't touch my money

I record my own sales and expenses myself, in the moment they happen, rather than waiting for a monthly statement to batch it all in later. When bank or Shopify data does get imported for reconciliation, the raw numbers are kept completely separate from any category assigned to them, so a categorization mistake can never quietly change what the account actually shows. No agent I run has a tool that can move money, place an order, or make a purchase.

AI can work freely in a Shopify draft, but it never publishes one

I've given AI a lot of room to rework theme drafts on Marie Gems' Shopify store, real layout changes and real section edits, without asking me first every single time. Publishing that draft live to the actual storefront is a completely separate line, and it stays mine every time, no matter how finished the draft is or how many earlier drafts it already got right.

AI can't send an email even if it tried to

The agent that checks my Flodesk list health has no send tool on that connector at all. It can tell me the Unengaged segment is growing, flag that a win-back is overdue, and even draft the win-back emails themselves, but the most those drafts can ever become is a doc I copy and paste into Flodesk with my own hands. That's not a policy I have to remember to follow. It's a wall the tool itself can't get past.

What about the batches that already run fully on their own?

Three Pinterest pipelines and one daily social pipeline are on standing autopilot now, but they didn't all earn it on the same timeline. Marie Gems' Pinterest pipeline and its daily social pipeline both proved themselves over weeks of clean, correctly-checked batches before I handed them that trust, Pinterest since September 9th and social since September 15th. Your Kind Mind's Pinterest pipeline built its own real batch history and joined that same standing autopilot on September 17th. This site's brand-new Pinterest pipeline is the outlier: I moved it to full autopilot after exactly one batch, the same day it went live, a deliberate, faster call rather than the same weeks-long bar the other two cleared. All of them still stop and wait for me the moment something looks wrong, a quality check that fails twice, an account that doesn't match, or content outside the normal daily shape entirely. A newer feature inside the social pipeline, Instagram carousel posts, is explicitly carved back out of that automatic lane until it's proven itself the same way.

The one thing I still finish by hand every time

Every short video I make gets edited at a snappy, sped-up pace by default, using whatever real footage is actually usable that day, even when it isn't exactly the clip a script originally called for. But the sound and the final touches always get added by me in CapCut before anything is scheduled, and I review and reposition captions myself before a final version gets made at all. That's not a gap in the pipeline. It's the part I actually enjoy doing.

If you're curious how that trust actually got built up in the first place rather than handed over all at once, code, craft, and create is the closest thing I have to an answer.
