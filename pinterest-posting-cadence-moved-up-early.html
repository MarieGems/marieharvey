Why I Moved My Pinterest Posting Cadence Up So Early

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · November 17, 2026

My Pinterest posting cadence for this site was supposed to move from 5 pins a day to 10 starting Monday, October 5, 2026. Instead, it moved up two days early, to Saturday, October 3, because I asked for it directly: "go ahead and do marie harvey to 10 now." Here is what that one line actually changed, and what it did not touch.

What was the original plan for the 10-pin cadence?

The 10 pins a day rate was decided on September 25, 2026, but scheduled to start the following Monday, October 5, not immediately. Plans like this usually wait for a clean start to a week rather than cutting into a day that is already half scheduled.

Why did the date move up instead of waiting for Monday?

Because I said so directly, and there was no real reason to wait once I had. The already-planned increase just needed someone to flip the switch two days sooner than the calendar said. Waiting for Monday was a default, not a requirement.

What happened to the pins already scheduled at the old rate?

Saturday, October 3, and Sunday, October 4, already had pins scheduled at the old 5-a-day rate, numbered MH-PIN-0079 through MH-PIN-0088. Those did not get deleted or pushed back. Instead, a top-up batch ran right after the change to bring both days up to the full 10, so the cadence change reached the two days it was supposed to cover instead of only starting with the next fresh batch.

Did days before October 3 get bumped up too?

No, and that was a deliberate line, not an oversight. Days before October 3 stayed at 5 pins a day. The new rate was never applied retroactively, because a posting cadence is about what goes out from a certain point forward, not a correction to history.

What actually had to change behind the scenes?

Three files, all updated the same day: the scheduling task's own rules, the Pinterest scheduling agent's instructions, and this site's Pinterest brand profile. All three needed to agree on the new number, since a cadence that is right in one file and stale in another is how a posting rate quietly drifts back to the old pace without anyone noticing.

Why record a one-line request like this at all?

Because "do it now instead of Monday" is still a real decision with a real effective date and a real top-up batch that followed it. Writing it down means the next look at pin history shows exactly why October 3 and 4 look different from the days before them, instead of guessing.

The bottom line on moving a planned change up early

A scheduled change is not locked to its original date just because that date was already picked. If the only reason to wait is that the calendar said so, moving it up costs nothing and closes the gap faster. I post Pinterest pins for this site through my own account, marieharveyco.
