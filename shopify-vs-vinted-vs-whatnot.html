Multi-Channel Selling: Shopify, Vinted, and Whatnot Compared

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · September 2, 2026

Most product sellers pick one platform and stay there. Marie Gems runs across three at once — Shopify, Vinted, and Whatnot — and each one asks something completely different of you. If you're deciding whether to expand beyond a single channel, here's what actually changes depending on where you're selling.

Shopify: the owned storefront

Shopify is the foundation — a fully owned storefront where I control the whole experience, from branding to checkout to SEO. Nothing about it depends on another platform's algorithm or policies. That control is the biggest advantage, but it also means every bit of traffic has to come from somewhere — Shopify doesn't hand you browsing shoppers the way a marketplace app does.

Vinted: resale marketplace dynamics

Vinted works differently. It's a resale and marketplace-style platform, and buyers come in with a different mindset than they would on a branded storefront — more browsing, more negotiating, more expectation that they might make an offer instead of paying a listed price outright. Selling well on Vinted means understanding that buyer behavior isn't the same as Shopify buyer behavior, even if the product is identical.

Whatnot: live-selling format

Whatnot is its own category entirely — a live-selling platform, where products get sold in real time during a live stream rather than through static listings. That's a completely different skill set: presenting in the moment, reading a live audience, and creating urgency in a format that has more in common with a live auction than a traditional online store.

Why run all three instead of picking one

The tradeoff is real. Running three channels means three sets of listings to maintain, three different buyer expectations to understand, and three times the operational overhead compared to focusing on just one. But it also means Marie Gems isn't dependent on any single platform's traffic, algorithm changes, or policy shifts. If one channel slows down, the business doesn't stop — it just leans harder on the other two.

This isn't a "pick the best platform" conversation, because there isn't one best answer. It's about matching the platform to the product and the moment, and being willing to run more than one system at once if that's what actually keeps the business steady.
