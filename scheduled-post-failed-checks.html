Why My Scheduled Post Failed, and 3 Checks I Added

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · October 20, 2026

A scheduled post is not a delivered post, and my scheduled post failed in two different ways in September 2026 before I treated it that way. The daily social pipeline for Marie Gems had two Instagram carousels rejected over alt text, and a Pinterest pin that showed as scheduled and then failed at its slot. A check caught a third problem, slide uploads saved with the wrong file type, before it failed anything. Each of the three now has its own check.

What does it mean when a scheduled post failed after saying scheduled?

Because "scheduled" only means my tool accepted the request. Whether the post actually goes out is settled later, when the platform fetches the images and publishes. The daily pipeline sends to five platforms: Facebook, Instagram, Threads, Twitter, and Pinterest, and each one has its own rules about what it will accept. In all three cases below, the step that looked finished was not the step that mattered.

How did alt text that was too long stop two Instagram carousels?

My scheduling tool rejected the first attempt for both the September 18 and September 19 carousels. The quality-check agent had written alt text for each of the five slides at 330 to 520 characters, and the scheduling agent joins the five into one string. The totals came to 1,645 and 2,248 characters against a limit of 1,000. Both were fixed by hand after they failed, and then I asked for it to be made impossible to repeat.

What check catches alt text that is too long?

Two agents now measure it. The quality-check agent caps each slide's alt text at 160 characters and a single image's at 500, then measures the joined string and fails anything over 950. The scheduling agent measures every Instagram alt text again before it posts and rewrites any that runs over 950 shorter, without cutting a sentence in half. The 950 line leaves room under the 1,000 limit.

Why did I check the file type of my slide uploads?

Because the same slides were about to go to Pinterest. When I asked for the daily social posts, carousels especially, to also go to Pinterest, a check before pinning found that the previous day's five uploaded slides had been stored as a generic web form file type instead of an image, because the upload had no content type header. Instagram tolerated that, and Pinterest might not have, so the slides were uploaded again as PNG images before pinning.

Uploads now always send an image content type, and the pipeline reads back the headers of the returned URL to confirm it. My tool's documentation does not say that Pinterest carousels are supported, so each one is checked after posting, and any fallback to a single image is reported to me.

What happened to the Pinterest pin that said scheduled?

The capybara coloring book carousel pin from September 18 was accepted as scheduled for midnight UTC on September 20, and then it failed at publish time. Pinterest timed out after 7 seconds while fetching the five slides, which were 4 to 5 MB each. A bodysuit carousel built the same way published fine, so it was a transient timeout and not a broken file. I spotted it before the pipeline did. It was posted again as a new immediate submission and went out.

What does the pipeline recheck now?

At the start of every run, the scheduling agent looks at Pinterest posts that failed in the last 3 days and retries each one once as a new immediate post. If that fails, it tries once more with the slides resized to 1080 by 1350. It also reports the previous day's Pinterest outcomes to me. A status of scheduled at creation time is no longer treated as proof that a pin went out.

What should you check if you schedule your own posts?

Look at what actually went out the next day, not at the queue. Keep alt text short, and add up the total when a post has several images. Confirm that uploaded files are stored as images. And search for any post that showed as scheduled but has no live link. None of that needs special tools. It needs a habit, or, in my case, two agents that measure, retry, and report on every run.

What rule do all three checks share?

Check the outcome, not the acknowledgement. Alt text is measured, file types are read back, and failed pins are looked for after the slot has passed. Frequently asked questions

Why can a scheduled Pinterest pin still fail?

A scheduled status only means the tool accepted the pin. The images are fetched when the pin publishes. One Marie Gems carousel pin timed out after 7 seconds while Pinterest fetched its five slides, which were 4 to 5 MB each, and it was posted again as a new immediate submission.

Why was my Instagram carousel rejected over alt text?

My scheduling tool joins the alt text for all five slides into one string, and it rejected two carousels whose joined alt text came to 1,645 and 2,248 characters against a limit of 1,000. My checks now cap each slide at 160 characters and fail any joined string over 950.
