DTF Business Starter Kit: What a Beginner Actually Needs

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · November 6, 2026

A DTF business starter kit needs more than a printer and a heat press: a way to measure your powder, a way to check your press is actually hitting temperature, and somewhere dry to store film so it does not curl before you use it. My DTF Business Starter Kit list on Benable has 16 items, and the ones below are the supplies I have not covered in an earlier post about the printer, the press, or the accessories.

What do you need to measure before you print?

Your powder and your press temperature, not just by eye. My Benable note on the Digital Scale for Measuring DTF Powder says eyeballing your powder ratio works until it doesn't, and that the scale keeps every batch consistent. My note on the Infrared Thermometer Gun calls it a cheap way to check your press plate is actually hitting temp, and says guessing wrong is how you get bad transfers. Both are on my DTF Business Starter Kit list. For the press itself and its size range, which heat press fits DTF transfers from 4 to 12 inches covers that separately.

What keeps DTF film and powder from going to waste before you use them?

Dry, flat, sealed storage. My note on the Clear Storage Bins for DTF Supplies says film curls if it is not stored flat and sealed, and that bins like this keep your inventory from going to waste before you even use it. The same list has the DTF Transfer Film Roll itself, a 13 inch by 328 foot double sided matte roll, and my note calls it the starting point for every DTF print, with the matte finish giving a soft-hand feel on shirts.

What is a Dtf Shaker Oven, and do you need one?

It is a 2-in-1 shaker and oven combo, and my note says it saves counter space if you are just getting started. That makes it a different piece of equipment than the standalone curing oven I cover in DTF printing supplies, which compares a separate printer, oven, ink, and powder setup. The shaker oven is the compact option for a smaller workspace, not a replacement recommendation.

Why does a pretreat spray matter for cotton?

Because cotton blends need it to make a transfer last. My note on the Pretreat Spray for DTF Transfers on Cotton says to skip it at your own risk on cotton blends, and that it is the difference between a transfer that lasts and one that peels after a few washes. Pair it with Silicone Parchment Paper for Heat Press, which my note says pays for itself fast compared to buying single-use parchment for every press.

What slows down packing day once orders pick up?

Hand-tearing tape. My note on Packaging Tape Dispensers says that once your order volume picks up, hand-tearing tape gets old fast, and that a dispenser speeds up packing day a lot. For the rest of what goes into packing and shipping a DTF order, my small business supplies list, sorted by job covers that from a different list.

What temperature should a DTF press actually hit?

For my own DTF transfers at Marie Gems, the real number is 320 degrees Fahrenheit for 15 seconds with a cold peel. That is exactly what an infrared thermometer gun is for: confirming your own press plate is actually at your real pressing temperature, not just that the display says so.

The bottom line on a DTF business starter kit

Start with the scale and the thermometer gun, since both protect every batch you press after them, then add storage, the pretreat spray, and a tape dispenser as your order volume grows.
