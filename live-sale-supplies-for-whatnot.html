Live Sale Supplies for Whatnot: Tags, Light, and Backdrop

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · September 24, 2026

Live sale supplies for Whatnot come down to four jobs: tracking what you sold, showing each item clearly on camera, making the space behind you look intentional, and packing each item after the stream ends. Marie Gems sells on Whatnot, a live-selling platform where products are sold in real time during a stream, alongside Shopify, Vinted, Etsy, and direct sales. This post covers the items on my Amazon Business Supplies list that fit each of those four jobs and what to check on each listing.

What are number tags for in a live sale?

When you sell clothing live, a number tag is how you and a buyer both know which piece is being discussed. The Jetec live sale number tags on my list are a 300-piece set of multicolor plastic tags made for hangers, and the listing says they come in both normal and reverse (mirror image) numbers. That detail is worth understanding before you buy. If your camera or app flips the picture, a normal number can show up backward on screen, and the reverse version fixes that, so test one on your own screen. The set was about $20 as I write this. Numbered tags also give you a simple record, because you can write each number next to its item in your inventory sheet before the stream starts.

How do you light items from above during a stream?

An overhead phone mount with a light solves the problem of showing an item that sits on a table instead of in your hand. The InnoGear overhead phone mount ring light on my list is a desk-clamp arm stand with a ring light, adjustable brightness, and 3 color modes, and the listing names cooking videos and calls among its uses. With the phone looking straight down, a folded shirt or a small item shows up close and flat. Check the arm's reach and the clamp's opening against your table edge before ordering, since a clamp only works on a surface it fits. It was about $28 as I write this.

What should be behind you on camera?

A backdrop is what viewers see every time you look down at an item, so it is worth choosing on purpose. Two items on my list work together. The Popoko backdrop stand kit is a 10 by 7.5 foot T-shaped adjustable stand, and the listing says it comes with a carrying bag, 6 clamps, and 2 sandbags. The Sugargirl purple sequin backdrop curtain is a set of 2 panels, each 2 by 8 feet, and its listing calls it a glitter photography backdrop. Compare the stated sizes: two 2-foot panels cover about 4 feet of a 10 foot crossbar, which is plenty behind one person on a phone screen but not a full wall. Sequin also catches light, so check how it looks on your own camera before you go live. Purple sequin is already familiar in my own photos, since a portrait of me holding an amethyst was shot against one, though I am not saying it is this exact curtain. The stand was about $34 and the curtain about $20 as I write this.

How do you keep sizes clear when you sell clothing live?

Put the size where both you and the camera can see it. The Wmiwulien clothing size stickers on my list are 4,200 round half-inch stickers on 28 sheets, running from XS to XXXL, and the pack was about $5 as I write this. Stick the size on each hanger or bag before the stream starts, so you never have to hunt for a tag while people are waiting. A size that is visible on the hanger answers the question before anyone asks it, and you never read a label aloud.

What helps with packing after a live sale ends?

A folding board makes every folded shirt the same size, which speeds up bagging and keeps a stack of orders even. The BoxLegend shirt folding board on my list is the Version 3 model in purple, about $17 as I write this, and its listing says it is for adults and children. I run every part of Marie Gems myself, packing and shipping included, so a step that saves seconds per item is one I pay attention to.

What is the simplest live sale setup to start with?

Start with number tags and a place to set the phone, then add the rest as you learn what your streams need. Tags come first because they are the one item that changes how you run the sale. The light, the backdrop, the size stickers, and the folding board each fix one specific annoyance, so add them when you feel that annoyance.
