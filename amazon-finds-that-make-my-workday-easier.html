Focus Tools for Working From Home With Kids Around

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · October 2, 2026

The three focus tools I would pick from my Overwhelmed Mom Brain Essentials list are the Soundcore Q20i noise-cancelling headphones, a BEAVERLAB visual timer with a planning board, and a brain dump notepad. None of them makes a house full of kids quiet. What they do is protect the short pockets of focus that a workday at home actually gives you.

How do you focus when the kids are home all day?

You stop waiting for a long uninterrupted block and build for short stretches instead. I work from home in Wichita, Kansas, running Marie Gems and Marie Glow Studio with seven kids in the house, and I said in how the days actually work that most days I do not get uninterrupted time. I get whatever pockets of focus show up, and I use them. The three tools below are aimed at making those pockets count.

Do noise-cancelling headphones help when you work from home?

They help with steady background sound more than sudden sound. Noise cancelling does its best work on a dishwasher, a fan, or a television in the next room, and it does much less against a shout or a door slam. That is a fair trade at home, because it lets you settle into a task while you can still notice a child who needs you. The Soundcore Q20i noise-cancelling headphones on my list are the pink pair. Check the listing for battery life and fit, since comfort decides whether you actually keep them on for an hour. For calls, my Pinterest Marketing Tools list on Benable has a separate pick, headphones for phone calls, and my note on it says client calls while folding laundry or answering another kid's question is the reality, so a headset that actually sounds clear matters more than people think.

What does a visual timer do for an adult?

A visual timer shows time passing as something you can see instead of a number you have to keep checking. It works for kids who need to see how long is left, and it works just as well when you are the one who loses track of time. The BEAVERLAB visual timer with a planning board pairs the countdown with a board, so the day's tasks can sit right next to it. Set the timer for one task, work until it ends, then stop and decide what comes next. Short, bounded stretches are easier to protect than an open-ended afternoon.

What is a brain dump notepad, and who needs one?

A brain dump is writing down every open loop in your head so it stops taking up space while you try to work. I describe my own brain as running 100 tabs open, and a brain dump notepad is the low-effort version of closing some of them. When a stray thought interrupts a focused stretch, write it on the pad and go back to the task. You deal with the list later, on purpose, instead of trying to hold all of it at once.

How do the three tools work together in one focus stretch?

Use them in order. Start with the notepad and write down everything that is currently loud in your head, then pick one task and put it on the planning board. Set the visual timer, put on the headphones, and work until the timer ends. When it does, check the notepad for anything that needs attention and decide what the next stretch is. A short routine like that takes very little time to start and gives every pocket of focus a clear beginning and end, which is the part that gets lost when a kid walks in.

Which of the three should you buy first?

Pick by the problem you actually have. If noise is the thing that breaks your focus, start with the headphones. If you lose the thread and look up an hour later wondering where it went, start with the timer. If your head fills up with reminders mid-task, start with the notepad, since it is the simplest of the three and needs no charging or setup. My Overwhelmed Mom Brain Essentials list has 7 items in total, and these three are the ones aimed squarely at focus.

The bottom line on working from home with kids

A quiet workday at home is not coming, so plan around the pockets you get. Headphones cut the steady noise, a timer keeps a short stretch honest, and a notepad catches the thoughts that would otherwise pull you out of it.
