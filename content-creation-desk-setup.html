What's Still in My Pinterest Content Creation Setup

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · November 11, 2026

My content creation setup for making pin graphics and client content is built from seven items on my Pinterest Marketing Tools list on Benable that I have not covered in an earlier post: a laptop stand, a wireless keyboard and mouse combo, an adjustable LED desk lamp, a ring light tripod, a desk mat for flat-lay photos, headphones for calls, and a paper planner notebook.

Disclosure: some links in this post are affiliate links, including my Benable link, and I mark it as sponsored.

What does a content creation desk setup actually need?

Good light, a comfortable typing setup, and a place to plan before any of it goes digital. Every pick below is from my Pinterest Marketing Tools list on Benable, 16 items built from running Marie Glow Studio's Pinterest and content work day to day. These seven are the ones with my own note that another post on this site has not already detailed.

Why does a laptop stand matter for running two businesses?

Because years of looking down at a laptop screen catches up with your neck. My Benable note on the Laptop Stand For Desk says running two businesses off a laptop screen for years will wreck your neck, and that I wish I had gotten one sooner. It is the kind of upgrade that matters more the longer you put it off.

What keeps a wireless keyboard and mouse combo on this list?

How much typing and clicking client work actually involves. My note on the Wireless Keyboard and Mouse Combo says client work means a lot of typing and clicking, and that a decent wireless set beats fighting with a laptop trackpad all day. It pairs with the laptop stand, since raising the laptop screen means you need a separate keyboard and mouse anyway.

Why do pin graphics and reels need a ring light?

Because lighting is half of what makes a photo or a video look finished. My note on the Ring Light Tripod says good lighting is half the battle on pin graphics and reels, and that it fixes bad lighting without needing a full studio setup. It is one of the cheaper items on this list relative to what it changes in a finished photo.

Is a desk lamp really worth adding next to a ring light?

Yes, because the two solve different lighting problems. My note on the Adjustable LED Desk Lamp says overhead room lighting is never the right color temperature for design work, and that a good desk lamp fixes that fast. A ring light is for the photo or video itself; a desk lamp is for the hours of design work in between.

What is an aesthetic desk mat actually for?

A clean, repeatable background for phone photos of pin graphics or products. My note on the Aesthetic Desk Mat for Flat-Lay Photos says a clean background under your phone photos makes a huge difference on pin graphics, and that it is way cheaper than a real photo backdrop.

Why do headphones make this list instead of just using a phone speaker?

Because client calls happen in the middle of real life, not at a quiet desk. My note on the Headphones For Phone Calls says client calls while folding laundry or answering another kid's question is the reality, and that a headset that actually sounds clear matters more than people think.

Why keep a paper planner notebook next to a digital calendar?

Because an app can crash mid-brainstorm and a notebook never does. My note on the Undated Daily Content Planner Notebook says digital calendars are great until your app crashes mid-brainstorm, and that I still map out pin themes and client content on paper first. Undated means it is not tied to a specific start week, so it works whenever you pick it up.

The bottom line on a content creation setup

Start with the laptop stand and the keyboard and mouse if your neck and wrists are the daily cost, or start with the ring light and the desk mat if better-looking pin graphics are the goal.
