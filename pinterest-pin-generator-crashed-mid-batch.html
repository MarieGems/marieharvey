Why My Pinterest Pin Generator Crashed Partway Through

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · November 25, 2026

My Pinterest pin generator for Marie Gems crashed on October 10, 2026, twenty-three pins into a one-hundred pin batch, and the cause traced back to a dedup rule I had only partly fixed three weeks earlier. Here is what the rule was supposed to do, what it actually missed, and the one-line fix that got the batch running again.

What happened when the batch crashed?

Batch-024, a 100-pin batch with 71 Christmas pins, stopped at pin #24 with an error that no unique design was left to generate. The pipeline's own safety check, built to catch a systemic problem rather than silently dropping a third of a batch, caught it and stopped the run instead of shipping an incomplete set.

Why did the pin generator run out of unique designs?

Because a "look" rule I had written three weeks earlier, on September 25, was missing one piece of what it was supposed to track. A photo-style pin's design is built from a palette, a layout, and an accent color, plus a background texture, like marble, grid, terrazzo, or a wash finish. The September rule tracked palette, layout, and accent as a pin's "look" so near-identical pins would not ship, but it left out the background texture entirely.

How big was the gap between the rule and the real design?

Six times smaller than it should have been. Counting only palette and layout gave 5 palettes times 2 layouts, or 10 unique looks, for one theme and design pairing. The real pool, once the six background textures are counted too, is 5 times 2 times 6, or 60 unique looks. Any batch with 10 or more photo pins sharing one theme and design pairing, which is routine during a Christmas-heavy run, was always going to hit that 10-look ceiling.

Why didn't this show up until a Christmas batch?

Because a Christmas-focused posting plan increases exactly the kind of repetition this gap could not handle. With a standing rule to lean heavily into Christmas content through December, a single theme and design pairing was asked to produce far more than 10 photo pins in one batch, which is the exact condition that exposes a 10-look ceiling instead of a 60-look one.

What did I decide not to change?

The Christmas content itself. Two other fixes were on the table: giving Christmas a second accent color, or capping how many pins from the same theme and design pairing could go in one batch. Both are real options, but both are brand and content decisions, not bug fixes, and each would have been a bigger, less reversible change for a problem that was really just an incomplete tracking rule.

What was the actual fix?

Adding the background texture to the look rule, one line of code, plus a corrected comment explaining what the rule now covers. The brand palette, the Christmas accent color, and the planned mix of themes in the batch were not touched. The partial output from the crashed run was deleted, and the batch was rendered again from scratch with the fixed rule in place.

What does this teach about a "shortcut" tracking rule?

That a narrower rule meant to approximate a bigger one has to be checked against everything the bigger one actually covers, not just what was top of mind when it was written. The September rule was a reasonable shortcut for telling two pins apart quickly, but it was only checked against three of the four things that actually make two photo pins look different. My AI quality check kept missing cut-off text, so I fixed it covers a related kind of gap, where a check that exists did not cover what it needed to.

The bottom line on an incomplete dedup rule

When you write a rule to stand in for something bigger and more exact, list out everything the bigger thing actually includes before you trust the shortcut.
