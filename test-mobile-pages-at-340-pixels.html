Mobile Page Width Test: Why I Check at 340 Pixels Wide

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · October 30, 2026

A mobile page width test at 375 pixels said my page was fine, and my own phone, at about 340 pixels wide, showed me it was not. After my assistant added a new button to my link-in-bio page, the Amazon disclosure line under it overlapped the button by about 7 pixels. The page had been checked at 375 and passed. Here is what went wrong, how it was fixed, and why the page is now checked at both widths.

What went wrong on my link-in-bio page?

My assistant added a new button to the page and checked the result at 375 pixels wide. The disclosure line under the button fit on one line at that width, and it sat right against the button's bottom edge. It saw that the line looked tight and let it pass. The cause was a spacing rule on the disclosure that used a negative top margin of 6 pixels, which pulled the line up toward the button. This is the same page I rebuilt as a scroll film in behind the build, so it has had its share of small bugs.

Why did a 375 pixel test miss the overlap?

Because the text behaved differently at a narrower width. At 375 pixels the disclosure fit on one line and merely looked tight. At my phone's real width, about 340 pixels, it wrapped onto two lines, and the pulled-up spacing turned a tight fit into an overlap of about 7 pixels. The test width was fine as a test. It was just not the width my phone uses.

Who found the overlap under the button on my link-in-bio page?

I did, on my own phone, and I sent a screenshot. The 375 pixel check did not find it. That is the part worth being plain about, because my assistant had looked at the tight spot and decided it was fine, which is the exact moment a check is supposed to stop it.

What changed in the disclosure's styling to stop the overlap?

Three things changed. The negative margin became a positive spacing of 10 pixels above and 4 below. Some side padding was added. And balanced wrapping was turned on, so when the line breaks onto two lines the words split evenly instead of leaving a single word alone on the second line. After the change the gaps were measured instead of eyeballed: 8.7 pixels below the button and 18 pixels above the next one.

The plain version of the same page was not affected. Its disclosure is an ordinary item in the layout with no sticker wrapper around it, so it never had the pull-up spacing.

What is my rule for checking a phone page now?

For any phone-first page, the layout is measured at about 340 pixels wide as well as 375 before it is called done. It is measured with real bounding boxes, the actual edges of each element, and not just a glance at a screenshot. And any text that looks tight against another element counts as a bug until a measurement says otherwise.

How can you run a mobile page width test yourself?

Open the page in your browser's developer tools and set the viewport to a narrow width, then step it down past your usual test size. Look for text that wraps to an extra line, and for anything that now touches the element next to it. Then measure the gap between the two elements instead of judging it by eye. A common test width is a good start, but the width of your own phone is worth adding to the list. If you build a lot of pages, code, craft, and create covers the skills behind my builds.

The bottom line on testing a mobile page width

A page can pass at one width and break at another one 35 pixels narrower, so test at more than one. If a layout looks tight against something, treat that as a bug and measure it. Since a phone-first page is opened on a phone, the narrowest phone you actually use is the test that counts. You can see what I have built on my Brands page.
