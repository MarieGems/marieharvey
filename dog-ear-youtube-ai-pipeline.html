The AI Pipeline That Builds My Dog Ear YouTube Videos

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · November 18, 2026

An AI video pipeline builds every video for my Dog Ear YouTube channel before I have looked at the topic myself. The task is called dog-ear-youtube-nightly, it runs on its own at about 3:10 AM, and by the time I wake up there is either a finished video waiting for my review or a clear reason, logged by the run itself, why there isn't one yet. Dog Ear is the reading and book-tracking app I built, live at dogearreads.com, and this is the real pipeline that makes its YouTube videos, start to finish, without me touching it.

What is the dog-ear-youtube-nightly task?

It is a scheduled task that builds one complete Dog Ear YouTube video a night, on its own, while I am asleep. It only runs while the Claude app is actually open on my computer, so it is not a server running somewhere I can't see; it is my own machine doing the work overnight.

What does the pipeline read before it builds anything?

Two files first: NIGHTLY.md, which lays out the steps, and LESSONS.md, a single checklist that holds every lesson learned from the videos built before it. That second file is the part I like most. Video 4 is not starting from zero; it inherits every mistake video 1 through 3 already made and already got fixed, because nothing gets to repeat a fix that already happened once.

How does the pipeline decide which video to make?

It picks the next unused topic off a running topic bank, rather than me choosing one by hand each night. The topics themselves come from real research, not a guess, which is the next step.

What research and scripting happens before a single frame renders?

Real steps, not a placeholder script. The pipeline runs a search-demand check, a viral-pattern check against what is actually working in the niche, a weekly content plan, and a scripting pass that writes five hook options and scores each one before picking the strongest. None of that is skipped to save time, because a script built on a guess is the fastest way to end up with a video nobody searches for.

What visual style does a finished video actually get?

One of a small, named set of style kits, mixed with my own real clips. The pipeline pulls from a handful of Brand Nat style kits, rotate fast, scrapbook, and editorial among them, and lays my real Dog Ear footage into whichever one fits that night's topic. It is not the same look video after video, and it is never stock footage standing in for the app.

What makes the pipeline skip a night entirely?

Four real conditions, all safeguards rather than bugs. It skips the night if two videos are already sitting in review and waiting on me, if another run is already active, if fewer than three unused topics are left in the bank, or if a heavy render has been running for more than 30 minutes already. Every one of those exists to stop the pipeline from piling up work I haven't looked at yet, or from quietly getting stuck.

What happens after the video is actually built?

An AI judge checks it before I ever see it. The judge pass can send the video back for a repair round if something is actually wrong, up to two repair rounds, and only after that does the pipeline stop and call the video ready for review. I get exactly one push notification telling me it's there.

Does this pipeline ever upload, schedule, or post a video on its own?

No, never. Ready for review is the end of what it does by itself. Uploading, scheduling, and posting are all separate steps I still do by hand, every time, the same boundary I hold on every other AI pipeline running across my businesses.

The bottom line on this pipeline

A video getting built while I sleep only works because every piece of it is accountable to something real: a lessons file that never lets a fixed mistake repeat, a topic bank that refuses to run dry without me noticing, and a hard stop at review instead of at publish. Frequently asked questions

No. It stops at ready for review with one push notification; uploading, scheduling, and posting a video are all still done by hand, every time.
