Why My Pinterest Pin Disclosure Line Kept Repeating

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · November 10, 2026

A Pinterest pin disclosure line was ending every affiliate pin on this site's account the same way, and a quality check caught it before any of them went out: on October 9, 2026, a review of batch-009 found that all 31 affiliate pins, plus 6 unrelated Site Page pins, closed their description with the same, or nearly the same, line. Here is what the check found, why the pattern kept showing up, and the fix I asked for.

What did the quality check find wrong with my affiliate pins?

Every affiliate pin's description ended with the same compliance disclosure sentence, word for word or close to it, as the very last line. On top of that, 6 pins that point to a page on this site rather than an affiliate link shared their own templated closing line. Batch-009 had 77 pins total, and the repeated line showed up in 37 of them.

Why does a repeated disclosure line matter across a batch?

Because Pinterest and readers both see the pattern across a batch, not one pin at a time. A single pin with a disclosure line reads as honest and normal. Thirty-one pins in the same batch that all end the exact same way read as a template instead of real writing, even when each pin's headline is different.

Why did the pin-writing agent keep ending every description the same way?

Because its habit was to always append the disclosure at the very end, as the default last sentence, instead of treating where the disclosure sits as something that should vary. This was not the first time a batch hit a version of this problem. An earlier batch, batch-004, had already run into a shared closing line across its pins, just not tied specifically to the disclosure sentence. The pattern was recurring, now at a bigger scale, 77 pins instead of a smaller batch.

What fix did I ask for?

Two changes to how the pin-writing agent handles the mandatory disclosure. First, the disclosure sentence should sit in the middle of a description, not default to the final sentence. Second, every pin needs its own distinct closing line from the first draft, instead of leaning on a later quality-check pass to catch a repeated one after the fact. The second point matters as much as the first, since catching a problem on a retry means it already happened once, and I would rather the first draft not create it.

Why not just catch this in quality check every time?

Because relying on a retry to fix a pattern means the pattern keeps forming in the first place, batch after batch. I wrote about a version of this same idea in the difference between using AI and building an AI workflow, deciding ahead of time who checks what, and what the first draft is supposed to get right, is the actual workflow. A check that exists only to clean up after a known habit is not as good as fixing the habit.

Is a disclosure sentence still required on every affiliate pin?

Yes, and that is not what changed. Every affiliate pin on this site's Pinterest account still needs the disclosure sentence somewhere in its description. What changed is where that sentence sits and whether every pin's closing words are allowed to repeat.

Did this fix go into effect on the next batch?

I asked for it the same day the quality check flagged the problem, as a change to the pin-writing agent's content instructions. I have not yet checked a later batch against the new instruction, so this post is about the fix I put in place, not a result I am claiming.

The bottom line on a Pinterest pin disclosure line

A compliance sentence needs to show up on every pin, but it does not need to be the exact words in the exact spot every single time. When a batch of pins all end the same way, that is a pattern worth fixing at the instruction level, not patching pin by pin.
