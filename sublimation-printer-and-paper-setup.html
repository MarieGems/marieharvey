Sublimation Printer and Paper Setup: What I'd Buy First

← Back to Random Thoughts
Marie Harvey · November 14, 2026

A sublimation printer and paper setup needs more than one printer: a second printer for backup, the right paper for the batch size, and small supplies that protect every press. My Sublimation Must Haves list on Benable has 31 items, and the picks below are the printer, paper, and press-protection side of that list that an earlier post about blanks for holiday gifts did not cover.

Disclosure: some links in this post are affiliate links, including my Benable link, and as an Amazon Associate, I earn from qualifying purchases, at no extra cost to you.

Which sublimation printer is on my list?

The Epson EcoTank ET-2800 is the main one, and the Epson EcoTank ET-2803 is a second option. My Sublimation Must Haves list names the ET-2800 without a written opinion from me, so I am naming it as a real option, not reviewing it. My note on the ET-2803 says it is a more compact option if you need a second printer for a dedicated sublimation setup, which matters once sublimation work is big enough to need its own machine instead of sharing one with everything else.

Do you need a special paper for sublimation?

Yes, and the paper you pick depends on batch size. A-SUB Sublimation Paper is on my list by name, with no written note from me. The HTVRONT Sublimation Paper for T-Shirts and Mugs is the budget pick, and my note says it is a budget-friendly paper option for t-shirts and mugs when you need a bigger batch on hand.

What keeps a printer running when prints start looking streaky?

A printhead cleaning kit. My note on the Printhead Cleaning Kit for Inkjet Printers says it is good to have on hand if you notice streaky or faded prints, and that it clears clogged nozzles fast. Keeping one on the shelf before a printer actually needs it is cheaper than scrapping a bad print run.

What speeds up trimming a stack of printed sheets?

A dedicated paper cutter. My note on the Sublimation Paper Cutter says it makes trimming printed sheets down to size so much faster than scissors, which adds up once you are cutting more than a handful of sheets at a time.

What protects the heat press itself during a sublimation run?

Two things, a Teflon sheet and butcher paper. My note on the Heat Press Teflon Sheet says it protects your press plate and keeps designs from scorching, and that it is reusable for tons of pressings. My note on the Butcher Paper For Heat Press says it is handy for covering designs during pressing and protecting your press plate, as a second layer of protection or a cheaper single-use option next to the reusable Teflon sheet.

What keeps the work surface clean while you press?

A silicone crafting mat. My note on the Gartful 2 pack silicone crafting mats, 15.7 by 11.8 inches, says they protect your table or desk from heat and mess while you work on projects. A Purple Desk Organizer is on the same list, and my note says it organizes all your sublimation paper needs, which matters once you are keeping more than one paper type on hand.

Is there a heat press on this list I have not already covered?

Yes, one with a note I like enough to quote in full. A press whose listing title reads "Heat Press, Affordable, Automatic for Small Business" carries my note, "Love it! and its purple," which is about as plain an endorsement as I have written on any item on these lists.

Is there a tumbler option I have not covered in an earlier post?

Yes. The UIRZOTN 8 pack of 20 oz sublimation skinny tumblers is on this list, and my note says it comes in an 8-pack so you are not constantly reordering when you need several done at once. My earlier post on the press itself, tumbler press for sublimation, covers 20, 30, and 40 oz tumblers from the press side; this is a blank tumbler to put in one.

The bottom line on a sublimation printer and paper setup

Start with one printer and the paper that matches your batch size, then add the press-protection supplies as you press more often.
